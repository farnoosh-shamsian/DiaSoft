Proposed frames (full definitions)

🆕 Guest_friendship

Two individuals (or their houses), the Guest_friends, usually members of different communities, are bound by a lasting, reciprocal and often hereditary relationship that obliges each to receive, protect, feast and give gifts to the other (or his descendants) whenever he comes to the other's land. The bond is created or renewed by hospitality and by an exchange of Tokens (guest-gifts) and is held to be guaranteed by a divine Guarantor (Zeus Xenios). Unlike Guest_and_host, the relationship outlasts any single visit and is symmetrical: either party may act as host on a given occasion.

Inherits from Relation_between_individuals; uses Guest_and_host and Exchange and Giving.

Core frame elements
Guest_friend_1One party to the bond, when the parties are expressed separately.
Guest_friend_2The other party to the bond, when the parties are expressed separately.
Guest_friendsBoth parties expressed together (e.g. ξεῖνοι ἀλλήλων, 'guest-friends of each other').
Selected non-core FEs
TokenThe gift(s) exchanged to found, mark or renew the bond (ξεινήιον, arms, a bow).
Origin_of_bondHow or when the bond arose, in particular inheritance from the parties' fathers (πατρώϊος, παλαιός).
GuarantorThe deity held to protect guest-friends and punish violations (Ζεὺς ξείνιος).
Home_communityThe community or land of one of the parties.
TimeWhen the bond was formed or exercised.

🆕 Honorific_privilege

A Holder is accorded a Privilege by a Grantor (a community, a leader, an army or a god) as public recognition of the Holder's status, merit, age or role (the Basis). The Privilege may be a material share singled out for the Holder (a prize from the spoils, the choice cut of meat, an estate) or an immaterial prerogative (a seat of honour, the right to counsel, funeral rites); it both marks and constitutes the Holder's honour, so that withholding or removing it is a dishonour. The Privilege is often taken from a common Pool (spoils divided by the army, the meat of the feast).

uses Giving and Judgment and Rewards_and_punishments.

Core frame elements
HolderThe person (or class, e.g. elders, the dead) to whom the Privilege belongs or is given.
PrivilegeThe share or prerogative itself; often incorporated in the target (γέρας).
GrantorThe party that accords the Privilege (δῆμος, υἷες Ἀχαιῶν, a king, a god).
BasisThe status, merit, age or service in recognition of which the Privilege is accorded.
Selected non-core FEs
PoolThe common stock from which the Privilege is taken (spoils, the feast).
Material_formThe concrete object in which the Privilege is embodied (a tripod, a woman, the chine of an ox).
TimeWhen the Privilege is granted.
MannerHow the Privilege is granted or held.

🆕 Apportioning

A Distributor divides a Resource (a whole or a stock) into Shares and allots a Share to each member of a set of Recipients, according to some Basis (equality, rank, merit, the Distributor's will). Unlike Giving, which profiles a single transfer from Donor to Recipient, this frame profiles the distributive structure: one source, many recipients, each receiving his own portion, and the fairness or arbitrariness of the allotment. Typical scenes are the carving and serving of portions at a feast, the division of spoils or land, and a god's allotment of fortune to mortals.

Inherits from Giving; uses Sharing and Fairness_evaluation.

Core frame elements
DistributorThe agent who divides and allots.
RecipientsThe set of individuals each of whom receives a Share; may be expressed distributively (ἑκάστῳ).
SharesThe portions allotted (μοῖραι).
ResourceThe whole or stock that is divided (meat, wine, food, prosperity).
Selected non-core FEs
BasisThe principle governing the allotment (equality, rank, merit, the Distributor's will).
OccasionThe event at which the distribution takes place (a feast, a division of spoils).
PlaceWhere the distribution takes place.
MannerHow the distribution is carried out.
